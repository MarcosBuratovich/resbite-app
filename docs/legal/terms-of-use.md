## 5. What Resbite does

5.1 Resbite helps you plan small get-togethers with people you know, and with people they trust. With Resbite you can:
- find ideas for things to do;
- create a resbite with a title, time, place and cover photo, or a group that repeats;
- invite people from your phone's contacts, by typing a number, from your connections, or by sharing a link;
- answer invitations with Going, Maybe or Can't go;
- chat with the people in a resbite, and send announcements if you host;
- connect with people you have met at a resbite;
- get notifications about your plans.

5.2 **Answering on the web.** If someone invites you and you don't have the app, you can answer on link.resbite.com after confirming your number with a code. This creates an account, and these Terms apply to it.

5.3 **What other people can see.**
- Anyone who has a resbite's link can see its title, the host's name, the time, the area, the cover photo and how many people are going.
- Everyone invited to a resbite can see the exact place, even before they answer.
- Only people who answer Going or Maybe can see the meeting details.
- Everyone in a resbite's chat can read the whole chat, including people who join later. In a group, everyone invited to the current week is in its chat, including people who haven't answered yet.
- People who have your number can find out that you use Resbite.

5.4 **How chats work.**
- Chats are stored on our servers. They are not end-to-end encrypted.
- We don't read chats as a matter of routine. Our team sees a message when someone reports it. We look at other information we hold only when we need to, for example to look into a report, to keep Resbite secure, or because the law requires it.
- A one-off resbite's chat closes 48 hours after the resbite ends. You can still read it. A group's chat stays open while the group repeats.
- We delete old chats after the periods set out in our Privacy Policy.

5.5 **Ideas are suggestions.** The ideas we show are for inspiration. Check that an activity is safe and suitable for you and your guests.

## 8. If you host

8.1 **Your resbite is your responsibility:** what you describe, where and when it happens, and how you run it.

8.2 **Describe it honestly.** Keep the time and place up to date. If plans change, change them in the app so your guests are told. If you call it off, cancel it in the app.

8.3 **You choose your guests.**
- People you invite from your contacts, by number or from your connections are invited straight away.
- Anyone who has your resbite's link can ask to join. You approve or decline each request.
- You can let your guests bring people. You choose how many each guest can bring and how far that trust goes. **People a guest brings join without your approval,** so only turn this on if you trust your guests' choices.
- You can remove a guest at any time. Another guest can't bring someone you removed straight back.

8.4 **Share places with care.**
- Everyone you invite sees the exact place, even before they answer.
- Put private details, such as a door code or a flat number, in the meeting details. Only you, your co-hosts and people who answer Going or Maybe can see them.
- Anyone with your link can see the area, so keep it general.

8.5 **Co-hosts.** You can make guests co-hosts. Co-hosts help run the resbite and can do most of what you can. Choose people you trust.

8.6 **Your chat.** As a host, you can send announcements and remove messages in your resbite's chat. Use these tools fairly. When a host removes a message, the chat shows "Message removed by an admin". That is the host's decision, not ours. If you think a host is misusing their tools, report them.

8.7 **Follow the law.** You are responsible for following the law at your resbite, for example on alcohol, noise, licensing and the rules of any venue. You are responsible for anything you charge or collect.

8.8 **Invite people who know you.**
- Only invite people who would expect to hear from you.
- Don't use Resbite to contact strangers or to collect people's numbers.
- Invitations you send by WhatsApp or Messages go from your own phone, and you choose the words. Keep them personal, and don't use them to advertise.

## 10. How we keep Resbite safe

### 10.1 How we protect you from illegal content

- **We make it harder to misuse Resbite.** Everyone must be 18 or over and have a verified mobile number. There is no public feed and no directory of people. Most people join because someone invited them, and hosts approve each request to join through a link. Our blocked-terms filter stops the worst words before they are posted (section 10.2).
- **We find out quickly.** Anyone can report content, with or without an account (section 10.4). Each new report alerts our moderators, and we read reports every day.
- **We act quickly.** When we become aware of illegal content, we remove it swiftly. We aim to act on every report within 24 hours. We remove an intimate image named in a valid report within 48 hours at the latest. We deal with child sexual abuse content first.
- **We stop it coming back.** We suspend or ban the accounts responsible. If a suspended or banned person comes back with a new account, we suspend or ban that account too when we find it.
- **We work with the authorities** where the law requires it (section 10.8).
- **We review our approach.** We assess the risk of illegal content on Resbite at least once a year, and before we make significant changes.

**How we deal with each kind of the most serious illegal content.** The Online Safety Act 2023 names the most serious kinds of illegal content as "priority illegal content". Our community rules forbid all of it (section 9). For each kind:
- **Terrorism content.** We remove it, ban the account, and report it to Counter Terrorism Policing. We act within 24 hours of a report, and at once if someone is in danger.
- **Child sexual exploitation and abuse content.** We deal with it before anything else, as soon as we see it. We take it out of view, keep the evidence the law requires, ban the account and report it to the National Crime Agency the same day.
- **Other priority illegal content**, for example threats, harassment and stalking, hate crime, fraud, selling drugs or weapons, encouraging suicide or self-harm, intimate image abuse and cyberflashing. We remove it within 24 hours of a report, and an intimate image within 48 hours at the latest. We suspend or ban the account responsible (section 10.6).

None of it stays up longer than these times once we know about it. Where the police or the law need it, we keep a copy out of view as evidence.

### 10.2 Our blocked-terms filter

The Online Safety Act 2023 asks us to tell you about any "proactive technology" we use. Our only proactive technology is a blocked-terms filter.

- **What it is.** An automatic check against a list of words and phrases that our team keeps.
- **When it runs.** Every time someone sends a chat message or an announcement, or saves a resbite's title or description, a note to guests, meeting details, or their display name.
- **How it works.** It looks for any listed word or phrase as a whole word, in capitals or in lower case. A listed phrase matches however many spaces or line breaks separate its words. If the text contains one, it isn't saved or sent, and the app tells you so. The filter doesn't keep a copy of what you tried to send. It doesn't report you, and it doesn't change your account.
- **The list.** We keep the list short and aimed at the worst words, so that people can still speak freely. We don't publish it, so that it is harder to get round.
- **Its limits.** The filter doesn't understand context. It may stop an innocent message that happens to contain a listed word. It can miss misspellings, slang and other languages. It doesn't check photos.
- **What we don't use.** We don't scan images, and we don't use artificial intelligence to moderate content. After a report, a person decides what happens, not a machine.
- If you think the filter stopped something it shouldn't have, tell us (section 11).

### 10.3 Your part

You help keep Resbite safe by following the community rules, and by reporting and blocking when something is wrong.

### 10.4 Reporting

- **In the app.** Use **Report** on a message, a person, a chat's menu, an invitation you haven't answered, or a connection request. Choose the reason that fits best, for example Illegal or dangerous, Child safety, Intimate image shared without consent, Harassment, Spam, Not appropriate or Something else. You can add a note.
- **A resbite's title, description or cover photo.** Report its host and say what's wrong in your note.
- **Without the app, or without an account.** Write to support@resbite.com or use the contact form at https://link.resbite.com/support. Anyone affected by content on Resbite can report it: for example, the person a message or photo is about, someone it targets, or the parent or carer of a child.
- **If you can't report in the app,** for example because a host removed you from their resbite, write to us.
- **Please don't send us copies of intimate images or child abuse material.** Tell us where it is and we'll find it.
- **Your report is private.** The person you report isn't told that they were reported. We never tell them who reported them, unless the law requires us to.
- **The outcome.** The app doesn't send you the outcome, because we have to protect other people's privacy too. You can ask us what happened.
- **Limits.** You can send up to 20 reports a day. Don't make reports you know are false.
- **How long we keep reports.** We keep a copy of what was reported, even if the message or the account is later deleted. We delete a report 180 days after we close it. If you delete your account, we keep the reports you sent until we close them, and then for 180 days, but we remove your account from them.

### 10.5 Blocking

- You can block a person from the same places you can report them.
- When you block someone, you stop seeing their messages and announcements, and they can't send you notifications. Any connection between you ends. They aren't told.
- A block doesn't remove someone from a resbite. If you host, blocking hides their messages from you only. To stop them for everyone, remove them from the resbite.
- To unblock someone, go to **Profile → Data & privacy → Blocked people**. Unblocking doesn't bring back a connection.
- Our team can see who has blocked whom. It helps us spot people who cause problems.

### 10.6 What we can do

If someone breaks these Terms or the law, we can:
- remove their content;
- warn them;
- **suspend** their account. A suspended account can't use Resbite: no reading, no posting and no notifications. The person can still sign in to contact us or to delete their account;
- **ban** their account permanently. A banned account can't sign in;
- report them to the police or the National Crime Agency, and keep information as the law requires.

**How we decide.** We look at what happened, how serious it was, whether it was illegal, whether it has happened before, and the risk to other people. We have particular regard to freedom of expression and to privacy.
- For the most serious harms, such as child sexual abuse, threats to someone's life or terrorism, we ban the account straight away.
- If an account shared an intimate image without consent, we suspend it at once, and may then ban it.
- For less serious problems, we may start by removing the content or giving a warning.
- We can act on behaviour at a get-together arranged on Resbite where we have good reason to believe it happened.
- If we suspend a host, their groups stop repeating while the suspension lasts. They start again if we lift it.

### 10.7 Telling you about our decisions

- **If we remove your message,** the chat shows "Message removed by Resbite". You can ask us why.
- **If we suspend or ban your account,** the app tells you that your account can't be used, and shows how to contact us. If your account has an email address, we email you the reason and how to appeal. If it doesn't, write to us and we'll tell you why.
- We won't give a reason if that would break the law, put someone at risk, or harm an investigation.
- **We usually can't restore removed content.** Removing a message deletes its words. If an appeal succeeds, you are free to post it again.

### 10.8 The police, the National Crime Agency and Ofcom

- We report child sexual exploitation and abuse content to the National Crime Agency, as the law requires.
- We may share information with the police or other authorities when the law requires or allows it, for example to prevent or detect crime, or when someone's life is at risk. We check every request and share only what is needed.
- We answer information notices from Ofcom, the UK's online safety regulator.
- Courts, the police and regulators can send requests to privacy@resbite.com.
- When there is a legal reason, such as a police request or an open investigation, we may keep information that we would otherwise delete. That includes after you delete your account. We keep it only for as long as the reason lasts.
