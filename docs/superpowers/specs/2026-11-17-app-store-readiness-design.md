# App Store readiness: the first public release

**Date:** 17 November 2026. **Status:** final draft for the owner's review. This is a gap analysis and a design. Nothing in it is built, applied, deployed or published.
**Builds on:** `main` at `db8a221`, and five research reports in `research_notes/App Store readiness/`, each read in full: `apple-requirements.md` (APL ids), `uk-eu-law.md` (LAW ids), `audit-data-inventory.md` (DI ids), `audit-user-flows.md` (F ids) and `audit-release-ops.md` (RO ids). On 17 November every row was checked again against the files by a verifier, and a completeness review added the items marked **CR**. Corrected rows now carry the verified evidence.
**Not legal advice.** The law report says a UK solicitor should review the documents it leads to before launch (`uk-eu-law.md:3`). This document keeps that advice (decision D-12).

## How to read this document

- **Row ids** (SI-1, DEL-2 …) belong to this document. Workstream deliverables (A1, B3, C2 …) point back to them. The report ids in each row trace every claim to its source. **CR** in the report-ids column marks a row the completeness review added; its code evidence was re-checked on 17 November.
- **Gates.**
  - **SB, submission blocker.** App Store Connect refuses it, or App Review is very likely to reject the app without it. **SB (house)** marks the repo's own release gate, a recorded decision, where Apple asks for less.
  - **LB, legal launch blocker.** A legal duty that must be met before the public release, even though Apple does not check it.
  - **S, should.** Apple or regulator guidance, or a known review or legal risk.
  - **N, nice to have.**
  - "(met)" after a gate means the item is already done.
- **States.**
  - **Done:** built, and live where it needs the backend.
  - **Partial:** built in part, or built but not live.
  - **Missing:** not built, or no record that it exists.
  - **N/A:** does not apply under the recommended scope.
- **Hosted state** comes from the repo's records only: `supabase/tests/hosted-baseline/migrations.txt`, `docs/implementation-status.md` and the `qa/` runbooks. Nobody touched the hosted project for this work.
- **Sources.** Short labels such as [ARG 5.1.1(v)] or [ADEL] are listed with their URLs in section 8. Law sources marked **(x)** were read only through search-engine extracts, because the regulator and legislation sites refused automated reads. Check those against the live page.
- **UNVERIFIED** marks anything not confirmed from a primary source. It stays marked exactly as the reports marked it.

## 1. Purpose and scope

**Purpose.** Prepare Resbite for its first public App Store submission, with every legal and mandatory flow and process complete. The plans for legal documents, app changes and the hosted rollout will be written from this document.

**In scope.**
- The iPhone app in `mobile/`. It is iPhone only (`mobile/app.json:11`, `supportsTablet: false`), with bundle id `com.resbite.app` (`mobile/app.json:12`).
- The Supabase backend in London, `eu-west-2` (`docs/implementation-status.md:30`).
- The link site `link.resbite.com` on Vercel, built from `links/`.
- The App Store Connect listing.
- The operations a public user-to-user service needs: support, moderation, data requests, breaches and records.
- Free, with no purchases, no ads and no tracking (`mobile/package.json:14-54`; DI-26).

**Out of scope.** Android (`mobile/app.json:21` keeps `com.resbite.preview`), a native iPad layout, paid features, and building the marketing site at `www.resbite.com`. The marketing site is not in this repo, and `/` on the link site redirects to it (`links/generate.mjs:85`). It belongs to the same operator and receives App Store traffic, so a check of its legal pages is in scope (WEB-8, F18).

**Primary market: the United Kingdom.** The testers so far are in the UK and Europe. The recommended scope is **UK storefront only for version 1.0** (decision D-2). This document is written for that scope. Age checks are not only a non-UK matter: Apple now asks UK adults to confirm they are 18 or over for some features, and whether that brings a developer duty in the UK is UNVERIFIED (ASC-17).

**If the EU/EEA is added**, these come in:
- EU GDPR applies directly (Art 3(2)). The privacy notice needs EU rights and EU complaint authorities (LAW-52).
- An EU representative must be appointed in writing (GDPR Art 27, LAW-53).
- The Digital Services Act adds a legal representative (Art 13, LAW-55), points of contact for authorities and users (Arts 11–12, LAW-54), moderation detail in the Terms (Art 14, LAW-56), notice and action, and statements of reasons for every restriction (Arts 16–18, LAW-57). The micro and small enterprise exemptions should be recorded (LAW-58).
- Apple publishes the trader's verified phone number and email on EU product pages (APL-56, LAW-60).
- National ePrivacy rules apply (LAW-59, UNVERIFIED for national detail).
- Data flows between the UK and the EU need nothing extra: UK adequacy was renewed to 27 December 2031 (LAW-61).

**If worldwide is chosen**, add these:
- Apple has blocked 18+ downloads in Australia, Brazil and Singapore since 24 February 2026 unless the user is confirmed adult. Developers may have their own duties (APL-52).
- US state laws apply: Texas from 4 June 2026, Utah from 6 May 2026 and Louisiana from 1 July 2026. Where the law requires it, the app must use Apple's Declared Age Range API (APL-53). That API is not built.
- Every other country brings its own privacy, consumer, online-safety and age rules. None were researched: not Argentina, despite the hints in `supabase/migrations/20261030130000_phone_key.sql:2-3`, nor the US or Australia (LAW-62).
- Worldwide is not recommended for 1.0.

## 2. Verdict

**Resbite cannot be submitted today.** Apple would reject it, and parts of it would break UK law on the first public day. It has nine submission blockers.

1. **Account deletion.** It is switched off and not on the hosted project. Its entry point would throw even with every gate on. Phone-only, web-only and suspended accounts could never use it (DEL-1, DEL-2, DEL-3, DEL-7).
2. **Phone-code test mode.** No SMS ever arrives, and anyone who knows `000000` can sign up or sign in with any number (SI-1). App Review also needs a sign-in that works without an SMS (SI-2).
3. **Privacy policy and Terms.** Neither document exists. There is no easy in-app link, and there are no public URLs (PRV-1, PRV-2, DOC-2, ASC-1, WEB-1).
4. **Contact details.** No support address is in the app, and there is no Support URL (UGC-4, ASC-2).
5. **Sample and beta content.** The Wellness tab shows sample data. "Beta", "development build", "Tester" and internal-note wording remains in the app, and "private test" wording remains in the live confirmation email and the legacy `/invite` page (CON-1, CON-2, CON-10).
6. **Moderation cover (house rule).** No backup moderator is named. Apple asks only for "timely responses", but the repo's own R-Q4 makes a backup release-blocking, and the draft review notes promise action within 24 hours from one person (UGC-5).
7. **The App Store Connect pack.** Privacy answers, the age rating with its 18+ override and the new descriptors, review notes for a first submission, screenshots, the listing text and the trader status are all missing (ASC-3 to ASC-8, ASC-11).
8. **The weekly-group roll job.** Without it a headline feature stops after the first week, and the review notes send the reviewer to a weekly group (OPS-1).
9. **Sign in with Apple on the hosted project.** The code is done, but the hosted Apple provider has no completion record, so a reviewer's Apple sign-in is unproven (SI-3, C11).

**Before the public release, the law also requires:**
- a named operator (DOC-1), with its particulars in the app, on the site and in emails (DOC-3, DOC-7);
- the Online Safety Act illegal content risk assessment and children's access assessment (DOC-4, DOC-5);
- a reporting and complaints route for affected people who are not users (UGC-9), and the 48-hour intimate image takedown once that duty is in force (UGC-10);
- a procedure for police requests, Ofcom information notices and legal holds (UGC-15);
- a DPIA, records of processing and legitimate interests assessments (PRV-5), with an Art 10 condition for the criminal allegations in reports (PRV-18);
- an enforced retention schedule (PRV-7), covering connections and refusals too (PRV-21);
- complaints, data-request and breach procedures (PRV-8 to PRV-10);
- the data protection fee (PRV-11);
- processor contracts, for every vendor including the mailbox and log tools (PRV-6);
- written instructions, confidentiality and MFA for everyone with admin access (PRV-17);
- a separate right-to-object statement for invited non-users (PRV-19);
- notices on the link site (WEB-2, WEB-3).

**Already in place:**
- Report, block, chat rules, a 37-term filter and moderator pushes are live on hosted (UGC-1 to UGC-3). The chat runbook's device checks have not yet run on a build.
- Sign in with Apple sits beside Google in code (SI-3). The hosted provider is unconfirmed.
- There is no tracking (PRV-14), and the contacts rules are followed (PERM-4).
- The technical basics pass: the export compliance key, the Xcode 26 toolchain, scene support and a 1024×1024 RGB icon with no alpha (CON-7).

## 7. Risks and open questions

### Risks

- **Test-mode exposure may already be a breach.** Since the open beta, anyone who knew `000000` could sign in to any number's account, or sign up with someone else's number (SI-1). The log review at switch-off must decide whether any exchange was not the owner's, and record the reasoning in the breach log (A17). The 72-hour clock runs from awareness.
- **SMS pumping cost** once paid SMS starts (`reports/Phone messaging providers.md:5`). Mitigate with CAPTCHA, Supabase's limits and per-number limits (F14).
- **Deletion is the riskiest build.** `activate_deletion_intent` predates chat, the Edge entry point is unwired, and two deletions at once can deadlock in groups (`qa/chat-release/README.md:1017`). Deletion must also respect a legal hold without blocking the request. Test only with disposable identities (`qa/b0-retention-proposal.md`, activation step 2).
- **CAPTCHA sequencing.** Switching CAPTCHA on before the build and the site send a token could lock everyone out of sign-in (OPS-9). Phase 5 orders it after the build.
- **Ban evasion and reused numbers.** A suspended person can return with another number (UGC-12), and a recycled number can open an old account (SI-11). Both are accepted for 1.0 with a recorded reason, not solved.
- **Laws with uncertain dates.** The 48-hour intimate image duty (UGC-10) and Apple's UK age checks (ASC-17) are both partly UNVERIFIED. The defaults treat them as in force.
- **The backup moderator sees everything.** On Supabase Pro the only workable role has full content access (PRV-17). A24 and MFA reduce the risk; the Team plan's Read-only role is the later fix.
- **One person runs moderation, support and operations.** The 24-hour promise and the complaint deadlines depend on the backup (D-6) and on alerts (F10).
- **Phone proof for deletion depends on SMS.** If C1 slips, phone-only accounts still cannot delete, and the submission waits.
- **Build-time values.** The support address and the URLs are fixed into the build. A wrong value means a new build. The B1 release test mitigates this.
- **Keychain items can survive deleting the app** (DI-22). Disclose this in A1 and A6. Sign-out clears most stores, and plan drafts are kept by design.
- **Push throughput** is about 25 jobs a minute (RO-27). Busy chats at launch could delay pushes.
- **No crash reporting** (RO-23). Faults in the field will be seen late.
- **Records drift** (RO-29). Plans must use the runbooks and the baseline, not the top summaries of `docs/implementation-status.md`.
