# Take Luma's guest path, leave its dashboard

The boss is half right. Luma is the strongest benchmark of the three, but the evidence locates its quality in the **guest path**: registration with just a name and email, a confirmation that adds itself to the guest's calendar, reminders one day and one hour before, and change notices that carry an updated calendar entry. Its creation form is also unusually polished. Its complexity is real but sits almost entirely on the **host side**: four management tabs, a calendar-and-subscriber layer, approvals, waitlists, tickets and check-in, all built for recurring community organizers. Only two sources, each written by a single author, call it hard to use. "Not very accessible" is **unproven either way**. Nobody has audited Luma and no user complaints turned up. The concrete defects are on its web pages: pinch-zoom is blocked, headings are shallow, alt text says nothing, one host colour sits just under the AA contrast ratio, and some themes animate. Apple Invites is the closest model for Resbite's audience and privacy defaults, and WhatsApp shows the lowest-friction logic for RSVP and change notices. Resbite already has the strictest invitation links of the four. Its best gains are cheap ones: an invite step right after Save, explicit required fields, a description field for the cover photo, private link previews, and narrower, plain-language notifications. Next come a guest list revealed only after a guest answers, a Maybe answer, a single fixed reminder and add-to-calendar. It should refuse themes, forwardable links, plus-ones and Luma's host tools. Most of the high-value items are small, and the most important cluster depends on B1 switching on push delivery.

## Luma earns "best" on the guest side; its complexity lives in host tools

Luma's reputation is well grounded. Its App Store listing shows **4.9/5 from about 19,000 ratings** and an Editors' Choice badge ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1546150895)). A February 2026 design critique calls the creation flow "an example of good design": clear signifiers, a submit button that stays disabled until the event has a title, and a short description under every setting ([IXD@Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/luma-design-critique/)). A product teardown credits Luma with folding a multi-page form into **one screen with date and time in overlays** ([ScreensDesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/luma-delightful-events)). The guest side is where Luma pulls ahead. A guest registers with **only a name and email, with no account and no sign-in**, and the confirmation email carries a calendar invite that adds itself to the guest's calendar ([Luma help: registration](https://help.luma.com/p/event-registration-process)). Registered guests get reminders **one day and one hour before** by email, SMS and push, and they can switch these off ([Luma help: reminders](https://help.luma.com/p/update-to-reminders-on-luma)). When the host moves the time or place, Going guests get an email with a **new calendar invite** and, on iOS, a push ([Luma help: updating](https://help.luma.com/p/updating-event-information)). Reviewers call it "light years ahead of eventbrite" and the invite process "simple" ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/luma-events-invites/id1546150895?see-all=reviews)).

Direct support for "complex" comes from **two sources, each by a single author**. A party-planning blog calls Luma "a bit difficult to use for first-time hosts" and says "the interface has a lot of options and it can feel overwhelming at first" ([party.pro](https://party.pro/luma/)). The Pratt critique finds that required fields are not marked. It also says "Blast" is easily mistaken for something separate from "Invite" and suggests renaming it "Announce", and it notes there is no clear home button and that hosts struggle to tell their own events from suggested ones ([IXD@Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/luma-design-critique/)). Luma's structure supports that reading. After creation the host works across **four tabs (Registration, Guests, Blasts, More)**, and approval, registration questions, capacity and waitlist are configured there rather than in the create form ([Luma help: creating](https://help.luma.com/p/creating-an-event)). Every event belongs to a calendar, which brings subscribers, team permissions, tags and membership ([Luma help: calendars](https://help.luma.com/p/luma-calendar-overview)). The waitlist starts only when the whole event is full and does not refill on its own ([Luma help: waitlist](https://help.luma.com/p/waitlist)), and multi-date cloning works only on the web ([Luma help: cloning](https://help.luma.com/p/cloning-events)). The guest-side complaints that do appear are about search on the Discover tab, not about answering an invitation ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/luma-events-invites/id1546150895?see-all=reviews)).

The strongest case for Luma is that this complexity is the price of serving a different job. Luma pitches itself to recurring hosts with calendars and casts Partiful as the tool for casual parties ([Luma vs Partiful](https://help.luma.com/p/luma-vs-partiful)), and the competitor Ticket Tailor makes the same point from the other side: Luma fits one-off casual events less well ([Ticket Tailor](https://www.tickettailor.com/blog/luma-reviews-summary-pros-cons)). Moving approvals and questions out of the create form is itself a choice that keeps first-time creation simple. Little independent judgement exists anywhere: Capterra shows **zero ratings** and G2 has too few reviews to score ([Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/10027468/Luma/), [G2](https://www.g2.com/sellers/luma)).

On accessibility, Luma calls its work "an ongoing effort, not a checkbox" and aims for keyboard and screen-reader support on web, iOS and Android. It also says plainly that it has **no VPAT, WCAG conformance report or Section 508 certification** ([Luma accessibility](https://help.luma.com/p/accessibility)). Its App Store listing declares support for VoiceOver, Voice Control, Larger Text, Sufficient Contrast, Differentiate Without Color and Reduced Motion ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1546150895)). Developers fill in those labels themselves and no one audits them. No audit, AppleVis thread or review that mentions VoiceOver or text size was found.

The only concrete defects come from reading the static HTML of a few public web pages:

- **Pinch-zoom blocked.** Every page ships a viewport tag with **`maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=no`**, which blocks zoom in browsers that honour it ([sample event](https://luma.com/htcvdmse)).
- **Weak page structure.** Event pages have only an h1, no `<main>` landmark, and `lang="en"` even on Spanish-language events. Cover images carry "Cover Image for {title}" as alt text, which describes nothing.
- **Unlabelled inputs.** The logged-out create page has time inputs labelled only by an "HH:MM" placeholder, and two images have no alt text at all ([create page](https://luma.com/create)).
- **Colour and motion.** One host-chosen brand colour measured **4.46:1 against white**, just under the 4.5:1 AA threshold for normal text, although Luma says it adjusts theme colours for contrast ([sample event](https://luma.com/htcvdmse), [Luma themes help](https://help.luma.com/p/event-themes-and-customization)). Luma documents animated themes ("twinkling stars", "falling green code", "pixel pets wandering the page") without saying whether they respect a viewer's reduced-motion setting ([Luma themes help](https://help.luma.com/p/event-themes-and-customization)).

Nobody tested the iOS app.

| Boss's claim | Verdict | Strongest evidence |
|---|---|---|
| "Luma is the best" | **Supported**, for its job | 4.9 from ~19K ratings; Editors' Choice; guests register without an account; calendar invite added automatically; reminders 1 day and 1 hour before |
| "Complex" | **Plausible for hosts, unsupported for guests** | Two single-author critiques; four management tabs; calendar and subscriber layer; no complaints about the guest RSVP flow |
| "Not very accessible" | **Unproven**; only web-page defects shown | No audit and no complaints; accessibility support declared by Luma itself on iOS; pinch-zoom blocked; shallow headings; title used as alt text; one colour at 4.46:1; animated themes |

The likeliest reading of "not very accessible" is **cognitive and visual busyness** rather than a screen-reader failure: dozens of options, more than 40 themes and text laid over images. The evidence fits that reading, but no source states it. Two cheap checks would settle the question: ask the boss what they meant, and spend an hour with Luma's iOS app using VoiceOver and the largest text size. Either way, the lesson holds. Resbite should copy Luma's guest logic and leave its host surface behind.

## Three theories of the invitation: a designed card, a web page, a chat message

Each app's idea of what an invitation is decides its fields and defaults.

**Apple: the invitation is a designed card.** The editor is the invitation itself: a full-screen background, a title in a chosen font style, then date, location, description and attachments such as a shared album, a playlist or a link. **Every field is optional**, there is an explicit Preview step, and drafts are saved ([Apple Support: create](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev1d1c7cb6b/1.0/ios/1.0)). Choosing a location adds Apple Maps details automatically and, once a date is set, a weather forecast.

**Luma: the invitation is a configurable page.** It puts the same basics plus visibility, calendar, theme and capacity on one screen. The event goes live the moment the host taps Create, and Luma then offers to invite guests ([Luma help: creating](https://help.luma.com/p/creating-an-event), [IXD@Pratt](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/luma-design-critique/)).

**WhatsApp: the invitation is a message.** The form sits behind the chat's attach button. The host fills in a name, description, start, optional end, location and optional call link, and the event posts into the conversation as a card ([SAYS](https://says.com/my/tech/whatsapp-can-now-organise-meetings-events-for-groups), [WhatsApp blog, Apr 2025](https://blog.whatsapp.com/new-feature-roundup-updates-to-group-chats-events-calls-channels-and-more)).

These choices have consequences. Apple's card invites decoration and puts the title over the host's photo. Luma's page invites configuration. WhatsApp's message gets the shortest form, but it can only reach the chat's existing members.

**Resbite asks for more up front than the others.** It sits between Apple and Luma: one editor in sections with drafts saved on the device. But it requires **four things: a title, a date and time, a place, and one or two categories**. Apple requires none and Luma only a title, and Resbite's server rejects an event without categories ([custom_events.sql](../supabase/migrations/20261007150000_custom_events.sql)). The requirements are defensible: a friend cannot decide without knowing when and where, and categories feed the wellness features. What the evidence argues against is leaving them implicit, which is the critique's main complaint about Luma's create form.

**Resbite already has an advantage none of the three offers: 11 curated ideas that pre-fill an editable event** ([Resbite context](../research_notes/Event%20creation%20app%20benchmark/resbite-context.md)). They do the job of Luma's themes and Apple's backgrounds, a good-looking invitation in seconds, without the risks that come with decoration.

On sharing, the apps differ most in who can get in.

- **Apple** offers a public link that anyone can open, with an optional Approve Guests gate, or a personal link per contact. People invited individually join automatically, and denying someone blocks them from asking again ([Apple Support: approve](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev48e9e39e0/1.0/ios/1.0), [Apple Support: invite](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev851dd16db/1.0/ios/1.0)). Six Colors found sending personal invitations one at a time **"more laborious than expected"** ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/link/2025/02/apples-invites-invites-you-to-send-invitations/)).
- **Luma** invites go out by email, SMS, WhatsApp or push. The links work for whoever opens them: they are **not tied to the invited email, so a forwarded link bypasses Require Approval**, and an invite cannot be resent ([Luma help: inviting](https://help.luma.com/p/inviting-and-adding-guests-to-your-event)).
- **WhatsApp** cannot invite anyone outside the group, and the event cannot be forwarded ([Benzinga](https://in.benzinga.com/content/38573671/how-to-create-and-manage-an-event-on-whatsapp)). People who join the group later do not see earlier events ([WhatsApp Help](https://faq.whatsapp.com/3855462068110340)). A September 2026 Android beta adds shareable links for people without WhatsApp, but it has not shipped on iPhone ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-introduces-a-new-hub-to-create-and-manage-events/)).

**Resbite's link model is already the strictest of the four.** Each link is a 256-bit random token that belongs to the first person who claims it. The organizer can revoke a link, which also removes whoever claimed it. Cancelling revokes every link, and claims close when the event starts ([foundation migration](../supabase/migrations/20260917190517_resbite_private_tester_foundation.sql)). The schema can tie a link to an email address, but the app keeps contact details on the device and relies on the first claim ([invitations.ts](../mobile/src/services/invitations.ts)). Today it is the approved-tester sign-in that stops a forwarded link.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Required fields and defaults | None required; Maps and weather added automatically | Title only (submit greyed out until set); time zone taken from the location | Not documented (press implies name and start) | Title, date and time, place, one or two categories |

## After the invite, notification rules matter more than fields

**Two of the three apps offer Maybe.** Apple has Going, Not Going and Maybe, with an optional note that the host and other guests can see ([Apple Support: RSVP](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/devc9d9cdbd5/1.0/ios/1.0)). WhatsApp added Maybe and **"Going with guest"** in April 2025 ([WhatsApp blog, Apr 2025](https://blog.whatsapp.com/new-feature-roundup-updates-to-group-chats-events-calls-channels-and-more)). Luma has no Maybe, and an App Store reviewer asked for an "interested/maybe" option ([App Store reviews](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/luma-events-invites/id1546150895?see-all=reviews)). Resbite records pending, accepted, declined and withdrawn ([foundation migration](../supabase/migrations/20260917190517_resbite_private_tester_foundation.sql)). Withdrawn means backing out after accepting. None of the other apps shows that distinction, and guests will only understand it if the wording is plain.

**Each app sets a different rule for who sees the guest list.** By default, Apple shows guests the list **only after they reply**, and since June 2026 the host can show it to everyone ([Apple Support: settings](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev7e7cef88f/1.0/ios/1.0), [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/23/apple-invites-app-gets-co-hosting/)). Luma treats a visible list as social proof behind a host toggle ([Luma help: guest list](https://help.luma.com/p/event-guest-list)). WhatsApp's beta shows confirmed guests the other attendees' names, shows phone numbers only for saved contacts, and shows the host every number ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-introduces-a-new-hub-to-create-and-manage-events/)).

**Plus-ones go two ways.** Apple caps them with an Additional Guests setting ([Apple Support: settings](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev7e7cef88f/1.0/ios/1.0)). WhatsApp's plus-one could not be switched off in 2024 ([SAYS](https://says.com/my/tech/whatsapp-can-now-organise-meetings-events-for-groups)), and its 2026 beta adds a toggle ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-introduces-a-new-hub-to-create-and-manage-events/)).

Organizer tools scale with each app's audience.

- **Apple** gives hosts a single broadcast **Note** to all guests ([Apple Support: note](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/deve337623d8/1.0/ios/1.0)). Hosts can remove guests, who are then notified ([Apple Support: manage guests](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/p7831yg2q6qn/1.0/ios/1.0)). They can add up to five co-hosts. A co-host must have replied first, can edit details and invite guests, and cannot delete the event ([Apple Support: co-host](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/h71m0jnjl5gj/1.0/ios/1.0)). A cancelled event stays visible with a crossed-out title, its RSVPs closed, and **can be restored**. Deletion cannot be undone ([Apple Support: cancel](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/devb73654655/1.0/ios/1.0)).
- **Luma** gives hosts Blasts, scheduled or immediate announcements that also appear on the event page ([Luma help: reminders](https://help.luma.com/p/update-to-reminders-on-luma)). It adds QR check-in with staff roles ([Luma help: check-in](https://help.luma.com/p/check-in-guests-for-in-person-events)). Cancelling **deletes the event page** and notifies guests ([Luma help: cancel](https://help.luma.com/p/canceling-an-event)).
- **WhatsApp** has one host who edits or cancels, and no co-hosts ([Benzinga](https://in.benzinga.com/content/38573671/how-to-create-and-manage-an-event-on-whatsapp)).

Notification logic is where the apps teach the most.

- **Luma** sends reminders on a fixed schedule. Guests can turn them off, and hosts **can no longer edit them**, so any custom wording goes out as a Blast ([Luma help: reminders](https://help.luma.com/p/update-to-reminders-on-luma)). Only changes to time or place notify, and only Going guests hear about them, which leaves undecided invitees uninformed ([Luma help: updating](https://help.luma.com/p/updating-event-information)).
- **WhatsApp** notifies Going and Maybe responders when the event changes or is cancelled, and posts a notice in the chat for everyone ([WhatsApp Help](https://faq.whatsapp.com/3855462068110340)). Its automatic reminder fired only at the start time in 2024 ([SAYS](https://says.com/my/tech/whatsapp-can-now-organise-meetings-events-for-groups)). The 2026 beta defaults to **one hour before** and lets the host change it ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-introduces-a-new-hub-to-create-and-manage-events/)).
- **Apple** offers push and email toggles for changes ([Apple Support: notifications](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/devd1a3c152e/1.0/ios/1.0)). No reminder feature was found in its documentation.

**Resbite's prepared notification queue is broader and blunter than any of them.** Every edit raises the event's version number and queues a "plan changed" notice for **every attendee, including those who declined or withdrew**. That happens whether the organizer moved the time or fixed a typo in the title ([custom_events.sql](../supabase/migrations/20261007150000_custom_events.sql)), and the wording is the generic "You have an update" ([Resbite context](../research_notes/Event%20creation%20app%20benchmark/resbite-context.md)). The better rule combines Luma's trigger with WhatsApp's audience:

- A change to the time or place goes to accepted and still-pending invitees, and to Maybe if it is added.
- A cosmetic edit updates the event page quietly.
- A cancellation reaches everyone.

Because delivery is still switched off, this costs a query change now rather than an apology to testers later.

## Resbite already leads on privacy and can lead on accessibility by removing things

Measured against Resbite's rule that an event is visible only to its organizer and claimed invitees, the other three each leak somewhere.

- **Apple**: anyone holding the public link can view and join ([Apple Support: approve](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev48e9e39e0/1.0/ios/1.0)). Apple does offer a sound control, **Remove Background Preview**, which limits the background image to verified viewers rather than showing it in link previews ([Apple Support: settings](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev7e7cef88f/1.0/ios/1.0)).
- **Luma**: a "private" event is unlisted, yet anyone with the link can register ([Luma help: visibility](https://help.luma.com/p/setting-your-event-visibility)). Its forwardable invite links get around approval. It does let hosts hide the location until a guest is approved.
- **WhatsApp**: in-chat events are reportedly end-to-end encrypted, but that claim comes from an undated PhoneArena article and WhatsApp's own blog does not make it ([PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/whatsapp-is-widely-rolling-out-an-event-feature-for-group-chats_id159937)). The beta link flow shows people without WhatsApp only the name, date and time until they confirm, then reveals the location ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-introduces-a-new-hub-to-create-and-manage-events/)).

Resbite's remaining privacy gaps are new surfaces that B1 creates. Links on resbite.com get previewed in Messages threads, notification text appears on lock screens, and nobody has yet decided who sees the guest list or the place note.

**Accessibility risk clusters where apps let hosts decorate.** Apple's only documented accessibility feature is a per-event **Background Image Description** for screen-reader users ([Apple Support: settings](https://support.apple.com/en-az/guide/apple-invites/dev7e7cef88f/1.0/ios/1.0)). Apple publishes nothing on VoiceOver or Dynamic Type in Invites, and its titles sit over the host's own photos with only a font choice to control legibility. That legibility risk is an inference; no review measured it. WhatsApp documents nothing specific to events. Luma's documented risks are themes, host-chosen colours, animation and text over cover images.

Because Larger Text, VoiceOver and Reduce Motion are already acceptance checks, Resbite can win by subtraction:

- category colours as fixed tokens checked against 4.5:1
- the cover photo kept separate from text
- no animated backgrounds
- native iOS date and time pickers
- RSVP status shown in words as well as colour
- no silently disabled Save button

That last point adapts rather than copies Luma. A greyed-out submit tells a VoiceOver user the button is "dimmed" without saying why. This is a standard accessibility concern, not a finding from these sources. The web fallback page that B1 adds for invitation links needs the attention Luma's pages lack: a correct `lang`, a `<main>` landmark, real headings, labelled inputs and **no zoom lock**.

**These conclusions rest on what the notes could reach.** No one used any of the three apps hands-on, and Luma's iOS app was not tried with VoiceOver. Apple's support pages were read from a regional copy because the US copy returned an error. WhatsApp's own "create an event" help article could not be fetched, and its events hub is an Android beta reported by WABetaInfo, not by WhatsApp. Luma's default start time and duration are undocumented.

## Twenty-four moves, ranked by what B1 can carry

The ranking weighs value to a small group of friends against cost, then groups items by the infrastructure they need. **Tier A** (rows 1–7) ships before or alongside B1 because it needs nothing new. **Tier B** (rows 8–15) rides on B1's push activation, links on resbite.com and two-person testing. **Tier C** (rows 16–20) waits for contracts in B2 to B4. **Avoid** (rows 21–24) lists things to refuse. Effort sizes are rough estimates against Resbite's current code: **S** is about a day or two, **M** is up to a week including tests, and **L** is longer.

B1's push activation unblocks rows 10, 11 and 19 outright, and it unblocks the parts of rows 16 and 17 that react to a change. Rows 10, 11 and 13 should land **before** testers join, because they change what the database stores and what people receive. Changing those after real use means migrations and retracted wording.

## Conclusion

The useful split is not Luma against the rest. It is **the logic that guests experience against the controls hosts operate**. Every idea worth taking from Luma is guest logic: an RSVP with almost no friction, a calendar entry that maintains itself, a fixed reminder cadence the host cannot fiddle with, and change notices that say exactly what changed. Everything that made it "complex" is host machinery that Resbite's small, private, friends-only premise makes unnecessary. Apple and WhatsApp confirm the same principle from opposite ends. Apple's best ideas are privacy defaults: the guest list stays hidden until you answer, and the background stays out of link previews. WhatsApp's best ideas are notification rules. Neither app's best idea is a field.

For Resbite, the boss's critique therefore works better as a design constraint than as a verdict on Luma. If "not very accessible" means overwhelming, the remedy is restraint, and Resbite gets most of it by refusing themes, host-chosen colours, plus-ones and settings tabs. The real product decisions are in a short list that is easy to overlook: which edits notify, who receives them, what the lock screen says, and what a preview reveals. Deciding those rows before B1 switches delivery on is the cheapest point Resbite will ever have to get them right.
