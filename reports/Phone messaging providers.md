# Pay a few pence per code, and let the host keep sending the invitations

Researched 3 November 2026 for the owner's boss, before the next build. Detail and every source sit in `research_notes/Phone messaging providers/` (`prices.md`, `whatsapp-telegram-and-uk-rules.md`, `resbite-context.md`). All prices are list prices read from the providers' own pages on 3 November 2026, in the provider's currency. This is research, not legal advice.

**The short answer.** A verification code sent by SMS to a UK mobile costs **about 4 to 6 US cents** at list price from any credible provider, and about twice that to Argentina. Verification products that bundle the code logic (Twilio Verify, AWS OTP, Prelude) cost about double a plain SMS, and Resbite does not need them because Supabase already generates and checks the code. At the beta's scale this is **tens of dollars a month**: 1,000 codes a month cost USD 44 to 56, and 10,000 cost USD 440 to 560. **WhatsApp works for verification** and is cheaper (USD 0.022 a code in the UK, 0.026 in Argentina), but it reaches only people who have WhatsApp and, through Supabase, only via Twilio or via Resbite's own hook. **Telegram works for verification only** (USD 0.01 a code, Telegram users only). **Neither WhatsApp nor Telegram can carry an invitation to a number that has never contacted Resbite**: Telegram has no channel for it at all, and Meta's policy forbids a first message to someone who did not give the business their own number and opt in. **SMS can carry it technically, but under UK law a message sent by Resbite that promotes Resbite is direct marketing that needs the recipient's prior consent**, and since February 2026 PECR fines reach £17.5m or 4% of turnover. The design already built, where **the host sends a neutral message from their own phone**, is the low-risk path and should stay. The build needs one provider connected through the existing Send SMS hook, a short privacy notice on the invitation page, and a retention rule for non-users' numbers. Nothing in the invitation flow needs to change.

Three things move these numbers more than the provider does. **Retries and resends** are billed each time. **SMS pumping**, where a fraudster triggers codes to premium numbers, is the main cost risk of paid codes; Supabase's default limit of 30 SMS an hour per project and 60 seconds between codes for one person, plus CAPTCHA and Resbite's own per-number limits, are the defence ([Supabase rate limits](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/rate-limits)). The contact-invitations spec already requires those limits before paid SMS goes live. And **Argentina costs about double the UK** on every provider, so the current testers are the expensive ones.

Supabase itself charges nothing per SMS. Phone sign-in is billed only as monthly active users (50,000 free), and the "Advanced MFA (Phone)" add-on at USD 75 a month is a different feature ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing)).

**Which provider.** The differences at Resbite's volume are a few dollars a month, so pick on fit rather than price. Twilio is the only provider Supabase supports for the WhatsApp channel, gives a free UK alphanumeric sender ID, and is the one Supabase's own hook example uses ([Send SMS hook](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-hooks/send-sms-hook)); it costs USD 56 a month at 1,000 codes. Sinch is the cheapest readable list price, USD 44, SMS only. The recommendation here is **Twilio through Resbite's existing hook**: one account covers SMS now and WhatsApp codes later, and the hook keeps the test-mode switch and its log. Vonage, the other built-in provider, could not be priced from this machine; it is worth a look from a browser before deciding.

## 2. WhatsApp and Telegram for verification

**WhatsApp.** Meta sells a fixed *authentication template* ("<code> is your verification code.", with a copy-code button, one-tap autofill on Android, keyboard suggestions on iOS 26) at **USD 0.022 per delivered message in the UK and 0.026 in Argentina** ([Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing), [authentication templates](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/authentication-templates/authentication-templates.md)). Twilio adds USD 0.005 a message; 360dialog charges EUR 49 a month per number with no markup; Meta's Cloud API direct has no intermediary. The limits: the recipient must have WhatsApp (error 131026 otherwise, and there is no supported way to check beforehand), codes go only to the person's primary device, and the business must be verified in Business Manager with its own registered number. Supabase's `channel: "whatsapp"` works only with the Twilio and Twilio Verify providers; a custom hook can also send to WhatsApp, but Supabase passes no channel to the hook, so Resbite's hook would decide per number and fall back to SMS when WhatsApp fails ([Supabase phone sign-in](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/phone-login)).

**Telegram.** Telegram Gateway delivers "automated messages, such as verification codes, to users who registered their phone number on Telegram" at **USD 0.01 per delivered code**, refunded when not delivered, and free to your own number ([Telegram Gateway](https://core.telegram.org/gateway)). The API takes a number, an optional code you supply, a time-to-live and a callback for delivery reports; it has no free-text field ([Gateway API](https://core.telegram.org/gateway/api)). It is not a Supabase built-in, so it would run through the hook. Its terms forbid "unsolicited messages to users who have not provided explicit consent" and "user enumeration", so probing which numbers are on Telegram is off the table ([Gateway terms](https://telegram.org/tos/gateway)). Separately, Telegram's sign-in is now OpenID Connect and can return a verified phone number with the user's consent; Supabase accepts custom OIDC providers, but this combination is untested.

**What this means.** Both channels are cheaper than SMS and both need SMS behind them. For the UK, SMS first. Where WhatsApp is the default messenger, as for the Argentine testers, WhatsApp codes would cut the per-code cost by about three quarters and arrive where people already look; that is the second step, through the same Twilio account, once SMS is live. Telegram is not worth building for these markets. **Inference** on adoption; the penetration figures were not researched.

## 3. Sending invitations automatically: what the platforms allow

**Telegram: no path.** Bots "can't start conversations with users. A user must either add them to a group or send them a message first" ([Telegram Bots](https://core.telegram.org/bots)); the Bot API addresses chats, never phone numbers; Gateway carries only numeric codes. The only Telegram route is the share link `t.me/share/url`, where the host picks the chat and sends ([Telegram share](https://core.telegram.org/widgets/share)), which is what Resbite does today with WhatsApp and Messages.

**WhatsApp: ruled out by Meta before the law is reached.** The Business Messaging Policy, last updated 23 September 2026: "You may only contact people on WhatsApp if: (a) they have given you their mobile phone number or username; and (b) you have received opt-in permission from the recipient confirming that they wish to receive subsequent messages or calls from you" ([Meta policy](https://whatsappbusiness.com/policy/)). A number the host typed in fails both tests. A first message must also be an approved template, and an invitation ("Join us at {{location}} on {{date}}" is Meta's own *marketing* example) would be classed as marketing, with per-user caps, quality ratings that pause templates, a 250-recipient starting limit for a new business, and a complete pause on marketing templates to US numbers ([template categorisation](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/templates/template-categorization.md), [messaging limits](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/messaging-limits.md)). The message would also come from Resbite's business number, never from the host.

**SMS: technically open, legally the question in section 4.** Any provider will send a text to any number. The constraints are the law and the networks' sender-ID controls.

**The current design sits outside all of this.** Click-to-chat (`wa.me/<number>?text=…`), the Messages composer and the share sheet open the host's own app with the text filled in; the host presses Send ([WhatsApp click to chat](https://faq.whatsapp.com/5913398998672934/?locale=en_US)). None of it touches the Business Platform, so Meta's opt-in and template rules do not apply.

## 4. The UK rules

**PECR regulation 22** is the core: a person "shall neither transmit, nor instigate the transmission of, unsolicited communications for the purposes of direct marketing by means of electronic mail unless the recipient … has previously notified the sender that he consents" ([reg 22](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/22)). "Electronic mail" includes "messages sent using a short message service" ([reg 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/2)), and the ICO reads it as covering WhatsApp and in-app messages too. "Direct marketing" is "advertising or marketing material which is directed to particular individuals" (now in PECR itself, since August 2025). The soft opt-in needs a prior sale or negotiation, so it cannot cover an invitee. **Regulation 23** adds that the sender's identity may not be concealed and a valid opt-out address must be given ([reg 23](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2003/2426/regulation/23)).

**The ICO on "tell a friend" schemes** (search extracts; the ICO's pages refuse automated reads, so open them before quoting): "Some organisations try to get round the rules by asking people to forward a marketing message to their friends. However, you are 'instigating' them to send that message, so you must still comply with PECR." "You don't need to give an incentive for it to count as instigating. It's enough that you actively encourage someone to pass on your message." "You must not rely on the soft opt-ins for 'refer a friend' schemes. You must obtain valid consent." And on the line between service and marketing: "General branding, logos or straplines in these messages do not count as marketing", but "a key factor is likely to be the phrasing, tone and context" ([ICO electronic mail marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/), [identify direct marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/identify-direct-marketing/)).

**Three scenarios, in rising order of safety** (**Inference** throughout):

1. *Resbite sends the text or WhatsApp itself, with "Get Resbite" wording or an app link.* Resbite is the sender, the content markets Resbite, and the invitee cannot have consented before the first message. Not lawful as a first contact.
2. *Resbite sends a strictly neutral message in its own name: host, event, reply link, nothing about Resbite.* There is an argument that this relays the host's message rather than markets, but no ICO text addresses it, the ICO's service-message idea is built around existing customers, and any growth element tips it back into marketing. Uncertain; needs legal advice, a legitimate-interests assessment and a data protection impact assessment before launch.
3. *The host sends from their own phone with Resbite's pre-filled text.* The text today is "{host} invited you to {title}, {when}. Reply here: {link}" (`mobile/src/domain/contactInvites.ts`): no promotion, no reward, the host chooses each person and presses Send. On the ICO's own words, instigation means encouraging someone to pass on *marketing*; a plain personal invitation is not obviously marketing material. Lowest risk. It would rise if Resbite added "Get Resbite" copy, rewards for inviting, or an "invite all contacts" prompt.

**The stakes changed this year.** The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 moved PECR onto the UK GDPR penalty regime from 5 February 2026: breaches of regulation 22 can now draw "£17,500,000 or 4% of the undertaking's total annual worldwide turnover … whichever is higher" ([DUAA s.115](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/115), [DPA 2018 s.157](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/157), [commencement](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/82/regulation/2/made)). The regulator became the Information Commission on 30 September 2026.

**UK GDPR applies whatever the channel**, because the invitee's number is personal data Resbite received from a third party. That means a lawful basis (legitimate interests, with the three-part test written down), **an Article 14 notice "at the latest at the time of the first communication"** stating where the number came from and the right to object ([Art 14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/14)), minimisation (the phone key's hashing already fits), and a retention rule with an erasure route for numbers of people who never join. Codes are different: a code someone asked for is a service message, outside regulations 22 and 23, and needs only the sign-up privacy notice.

**Other layers**, which bite only if the invitation is Resbite's marketing: the CAP Code's requirement that marketing texts carry the marketer's name and an opt-out address ([CAP Code s.10](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/10.html)), the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act ban on "persistent and unwanted solicitations" ([DMCCA Sch 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20)), and the E-Commerce Regulations' identification rule. On the network side, Ofcom decided against a mandatory sender-ID registry and instead places Know-Your-Customer and sender-ID checks on operators and aggregators; its July 2026 statement could not be opened from this machine, so the final dates are unconfirmed. Possessing a SIM farm has been an offence since 29 October 2026 ([Crime and Policing Act 2026 s.151](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/20/section/151)), which matters only when vetting a cheap SMS supplier.

## 5. What this means for the build

**Keep the invitation design.** The host sends; the text stays neutral and editable; no rewards, no bulk prompts, and no follow-up to non-users from Resbite's side. This is both the cheapest option (zero per invitation) and the one the rules favour.

**Connect one SMS provider through the hook.** Twilio recommended, Sinch if price wins. The hook is a Postgres function today; calling a provider from it means either an HTTP hook (an Edge Function that carries the test-mode logic) or the Postgres hook queuing and `pg_net` calling out. That is a small plan of its own, with the Auth dashboard steps that are still pending (phone provider on, hook selected, OTP length 6, expiry 600 seconds, rate limits raised) and the abuse limits the spec already requires.

**Two small additions.** A short privacy notice on `link.resbite.com/i/<code>` (who Resbite is, "your friend gave us your number", why, how long, how to object), and a retention rule that deletes a non-user's number when the event ends or they decline. Both are cheap and needed whatever channel carries the invitation.

**Later, if volume grows.** WhatsApp codes through the same Twilio account, SMS as fallback, for markets where WhatsApp is the default. And if the business ever wants Resbite to message invitees itself, the compliant shape is **opt-in first**: a "send me updates on WhatsApp" choice on the invitation page, after which utility messages about that event are allowed.
