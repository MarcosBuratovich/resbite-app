Can WhatsApp, Telegram or SMS send invitations automatically?
Telegram has no path. Meta's policy forbids it. SMS can, but a Resbite-sent invitation that promotes Resbite needs prior consent under UK law. The host sending from their own phone, as built, is the safe design.

2. Why Twilio, when Sinch is cheaper

On price alone Twilio loses. Its UK list price, USD 0.056 a message, is the highest of the four providers whose prices could be read, and Argentina at USD 0.1034 is 14% above Sinch's 0.0906. At 1,000 UK codes a month the whole gap between Twilio and Sinch is about USD 12. Three things that are not price decide it.

WhatsApp through the same account. Supabase's phone sign-in can deliver codes on WhatsApp, where a code costs USD 0.022 in the UK and 0.026 in Argentina against 0.056 and 0.1034 by SMS, but only when the provider is Twilio or Twilio Verify. For the Argentine testers, where WhatsApp is the default messenger, that halves the per-code cost later without changing provider.

A free, published UK sender. Twilio's UK alphanumeric sender ID (RESBITE on the lock screen) is free and documented as one-way. Sinch, AWS and Plivo do not publish what a sender ID costs or how to get one; AWS says a UK number takes about a week to provision.

Documented twice by Supabase. Twilio is a built-in provider, and it is the provider Supabase's own Send SMS hook example is written for. Both ways of connecting are documented; nobody has to translate.

Against it. The highest UK list price of the readable four; a note that "additional carrier fees may apply" without a published table (Sinch has the same note); and a per-message markup of USD 0.005 on WhatsApp. None of these moves the bill by more than a few dollars a month at the beta's volume.

What would change the choice. If price is the only criterion, Sinch: SMS only, through the hook, carrier fees also unpublished. If the company already runs on AWS, AWS End User Messaging at USD 0.05. Vonage is the other Supabase built-in and its price pages refuse non-browser reads, so it deserves one look from a browser before the account is opened.

What the prices include, and what they don't

  • One segment. A code is one SMS segment (up to 160 GSM characters). An invitation with a link often runs to two.
  • Carrier fees. Sinch: "carrier fees apply", amounts not published. Twilio: "additional carrier fees may apply", no UK table. AWS and Plivo list none.
  • Sender. Twilio's UK alphanumeric sender is free, one-way. Prelude's needs a free registration. Sinch, AWS, Plivo, Vonage, Bird and Telnyx do not publish a price.
  • Numbers. A UK number per month: Plivo USD 0.85 local / 0.90 mobile, Twilio 1.15 / 2.50, AWS 2.00 (about a week to provision). Argentina on AWS: a support case.
  • Argentina. Plivo's "from USD 0.0795" rises to 0.14 on some networks; Sinch's Nextel rate is USD 0.018. The other providers publish one national rate.
  • Discounts. Twilio and Twilio Verify offer committed-use discounts only through sales (Verify from 100,000 a month). None of this matters at the beta's volume.

6. WhatsApp and Telegram, as providers

Both messengers sell verification codes, both are cheaper than SMS, and both reach only the people who have the app, so SMS stays behind them. Neither carries a first message to a number that never contacted Resbite.

WhatsApp is sold by Meta through its Business Platform at USD 0.022 a code in the UK and 0.026 in Argentina, per delivered message. Three routes buy it: Meta's Cloud API directly, with no markup but Resbite's own integration; Twilio, at USD 0.005 a message on top, integrated with Supabase; or a reseller such as 360dialog at EUR 49 a month per number with no markup, which beats Twilio's markup only above roughly 10,000 WhatsApp messages a month. The business must be verified by Meta with its own registered number, and a new account starts with a 250-recipient limit.

Telegram Gateway sells codes at USD 0.01, refunded when not delivered, prepaid, to people who registered the number on Telegram. It carries numbers only, no free text, and its terms forbid unsolicited messages and user enumeration. Not a Supabase built-in; it is not worth building for these two markets.

What this means. For the UK, SMS first. Where WhatsApp is the default messenger, as for the Argentine testers, WhatsApp codes would cut the per-code cost by about three quarters and arrive where people already look; that is the second step, through the same Twilio account. Adoption figures by country were not researched.

    The rule that decides it

    "A person shall neither transmit, nor instigate the transmission of, unsolicited communications for the purposes of direct marketing by means of electronic mail unless the recipient of the electronic mail has previously notified the sender that he consents for the time being to such communications being sent by, or at the instigation of, the sender."

    Privacy and Electronic Communications (EC Directive) Regulations 2003, regulation 22(2). "Electronic mail" includes SMS; the regulator reads it as covering WhatsApp and in-app messages too.

    Regulation 23 adds that a marketing message may not hide who sent it and must give an opt-out address. The ICO on "tell a friend" schemes (search extracts; the ICO's pages refuse automated reads, so open them before quoting): asking people to forward a marketing message is "instigating" it, "you don't need to give an incentive for it to count", and refer-a-friend schemes cannot rely on the soft opt-in. On the line between service and marketing: "general branding, logos or straplines in these messages do not count as marketing", but "a key factor is likely to be the phrasing, tone and context".

    UK GDPR applies whatever the channel, because the invitee's number is personal data Resbite received from a third party: a lawful basis written down, an Article 14 notice "at the latest at the time of the first communication", minimisation, and a retention rule for numbers of people who never join. Codes are different: a code someone asked for is a service message outside regulations 22 and 23.

    What a breach can cost since 5 February 2026

    The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 moved PECR onto the UK GDPR penalty scale: the higher of a fixed sum or a share of worldwide turnover.

    8. What happens next

    • Open a Twilio account, register the UK alphanumeric sender, and connect it behind Resbite's existing code-sending function. That is a small piece of work; the test mode used by the testers stays as a switch.
    • Keep the invitation design. The host sends; the text stays neutral and editable; no rewards, no "invite all contacts", no follow-up to non-users from Resbite.
    • Two small additions for the invitee's data: a short privacy notice on the invitation page and a retention rule for numbers of people who never join. Needed whatever channel carries the invitation.
    • Later, if volume grows: WhatsApp codes through the same Twilio account, SMS as fallback, for markets where WhatsApp is the default. And if Resbite ever wants to message invitees itself, opt-in first: a "send me updates on WhatsApp" choice on the invitation page.
    What could not be verified from the dev box.
    • ICO and Ofcom pages refuse automated reads: every ICO quotation here is a search-engine extract of the page named.
    • Vonage's UK and Argentina SMS prices; Bird, Telnyx and Infobip are quote-only or unreadable.
    • Twilio's and Sinch's carrier fees are referred to but not published; budget the list price and check the first invoice.
    • Whether a strictly neutral invitation sent by Resbite itself counts as direct marketing has no ICO text on point: a question for a lawyer.
